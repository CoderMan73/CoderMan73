# Last Updated: 10/10/2026.

No AI was used in the creation of this document. You can probably tell, lol, sorry, I'm quite lazy. They said a lazy programmer is a good one though :P

I got into computer science because I thought it was fun, something I was kind of good at, and paid well. And as I've gotten older and AI use as become more commonplace, many things have changed.

Computer science just isn't quite what it used to be. I'll put it plainly, AI removed a lot of the enjoyment and passion from programming for me. I could cover my eyes and ears and simply pretend AI does not exist,
and many people are doing that. I don't blame, judge, or say they are wrong for that. Simply put, our interests are aligned differently. I still enjoy putting together a program for fun, but the existence
of these tools is a fact in the back of my head at all times which constantly reminds me that whatever project I want to create could likely be created with AI much faster.

Given this, I've come to a resolution in my head. That is, I will not use AI tools in my personal life, as programming is not my personal life. It is my method of pursuing a career, financial gain, and a lifeline in the
capitalist society we exist within. 

I realize this will be quite troubling in the event that I do want to program something for my personal life, but for now, my interests of everything being applicable to my resume save me from thinking about that moral 
dilemma.

I do not use ChatGPT to make shopping lists. I do not use Claude to edit pictures for me. I try not to use Google AI Search, but Google is doing their best to lobotomize their search engine for everyone not using it.
I could talk your ear off and be on this soapbox forever and talk about the various good things AI is doing, but all that is just my personal opinions, and doesn't really matter. The bottom line I want to get across
is basically this: I don't use AI in my personal life. I only use AI for software engineering. If one day, through some string of fate, I end up not needing a source of income that relies on software engineering, I would
probably never use AI again. The unfortunate exception that I've had to come to terms with is if I ever program for personal joy again. In that case it obviously depends on if the programming is a means to an end or a part
of the final product. But that's not important. What's important is the reality we live in, and as far as I am aware (please feel free to tell me if this is not true), but it seems that software engineers that are being
hired are using AI, and as a software engineer who wants to be/stay hired, I need to keep up with the industry standards.

I don't mind talking about the ethics of AI, but honestly, it's all quite tiring for me. The conversations essentially go in the same directions each time. I would link articles about the negative impacts and all the bad
things about AI, and then also link about some of the positive parts of AI, but I'm sure whatever articles I link would be outdated not even a week from now. So I'll let you do your own research and form your own opinion.

I'm against generative AI "art" of all kinds, whether it be music, drawing, or any other art form. 

And I also just want a job without all this cognitive dissonance. Unfortunately, capitalism forces all of us to make hard decisions with our life (income) on the line...

I'm interested in anyone who reads this document's thoughts as well, so feel free to contact me somehow and tell me. I don't think I have any contacts on my Github though.
